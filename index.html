9 · drift

The picks have become more American

The share of each year's picks that are American films, against the American share of the shelf that year. Early on the two track each other. Since 2016 the picks run a third to a half above the shelf. The shelf did not change that much. Who was invited probably did; this data can't say.

10 · smaller things

Long films are fine; short ones aren't

Share of films by running time, shelf against picks. Films under ninety minutes are taken well below their share. Films over two hours are taken slightly above it. Black-and-white is taken a little below its share of the shelf, less than the age of the films would suggest.

method

How this was built

Sources. Each visit's picks come from the shop collection page Criterion publishes for it (criterion.com/closet-picks): visits, to . Film details, cast and credits come from each release's page on criterion.com, joined by spine number. Guests who visited together count as one visit; guests who returned count twice.

The shelf as it stood. Criterion numbers releases in order, so the shelf at a visit is approximated by the third-highest spine seen in any pick up to that date. picks sit ahead of this proxy because Criterion later released a film a guest had asked for and added it to the old page. Films sold only inside box sets are excluded from per-film counts.

Tested and not found. Whether a film's pick rate rises after a widely viewed guest takes it (no: 1.3 per hundred visits before, 1.0 after). Whether September–October visits, festival season in New York, differ in what they take (they don't). Whether any two visits share a shelf: of possible pairs of visits, share two or more picks, which is why the page above gives you one film rather than a twin.

Limits. The shop page lists what is in print, so a few early visits are shorter than their videos. Nothing here says why anyone picked anything. The notebook with every table is in the repository.
